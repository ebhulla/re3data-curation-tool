Re3data indexes more than 3,500 research data repositories. Its records are curated by hand, often once, while the repositories keep changing, so metadata becomes inconsistent, incomplete or out of date, and nobody has measured by how much. We present (1) a first measurement of metadata accuracy in re3data and (2) an AI audit engine that proposes corrections a human can verify. For [X] repositories we captured each website and annotated [X] fields against it as a gold standard; [X%] of current field values disagree with the website, more so in older records. For each field, the engine retrieves evidence from the captured pages and returns a verdict (confirmed, contradicted, new information, no evidence), a proposed value and its source. It reached [precision/recall] on contradicted fields and [X%] accuracy on repository typology, versus [Y%] for a fine-tuned classifier. Showing reviewers only uncertain or contradicted fields cuts the fields needing review by [X%]. Institution names need a separate step, because true duplicates look like legitimate hierarchies, such as a research center inside a university. On 2,290 LLM-judged name pairs (82 held out, no institution in two folds), fuzzy matching (F1 0.81) and sentence embeddings (F1 0.82) both find duplicates but fail in opposite ways: embeddings merge sub-units into their parents, and fine-tuning did not fix this. We discuss fitting this evidence-backed audit into the re3data editorial workflow while keeping human judgement.
