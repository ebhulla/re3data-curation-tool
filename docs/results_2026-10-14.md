# Preliminary results, 2026-10-14

Task: decide whether two institution names are the SAME institution (judge label `duplicate`) or not
(`hierarchy` or `unrelated`). Labels come from the swap-checked LLM judge (Groq, gpt-oss-120b). Pairs where the two
orderings disagreed, or the judge said `uncertain`, are dropped.

Data: 2,290 judged pairs (600 random/ROR-based + 1,690 mined by embedding nearest neighbors), 1,591 usable after the
leakage-safe split (`split_pairs.py`: no institution name or ROR cluster appears in two splits).
Threshold for each method is chosen on train, applied unchanged to test.

| Split | Pairs | Duplicates |
|---|---|---|
| train | 1,459 | 270 |
| val | 50 | 18 |
| test | 82 | 23 |

## Test results (82 pairs, 23 true duplicates)

| Method | Precision | Recall | F1 | AP | mean score: duplicate | hierarchy | unrelated |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact (normalized equality) | 1.00 | 0.39 | 0.56 | 0.56 | 0.39 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| substring (naive rule) | 0.41 | 0.61 | 0.49 | 0.36 | 0.61 | 0.69 | 0.00 |
| fuzzy (rapidfuzz token_sort) | 0.89 | 0.74 | 0.81 | 0.94 | 0.93 | 0.70 | 0.54 |
| MiniLM, off the shelf | 0.77 | 0.87 | 0.82 | 0.91 | 0.96 | 0.78 | 0.56 |
| MiniLM, fine-tuned (1 seed) | 0.71 | 0.87 | 0.78 | 0.88 | 0.94 | 0.69 | 0.43 |

## What the numbers support
- The substring rule cannot separate duplicates from hierarchies: it scores hierarchy pairs (0.69) above duplicates (0.61).
- Fuzzy matching and off-the-shelf MiniLM are close (F1 0.81 vs 0.82); fuzzy has the higher AP (0.94 vs 0.91).
- Fine-tuning (one seed) widened the average gap between duplicates and hierarchies (0.25 vs 0.18) but did not improve
  F1 or AP on the test set.

## What they do not support
- Any claim that fine-tuning helps or hurts: the test set has 23 duplicates, so one pair moves recall by about 4 points,
  and the differences above (0.03 to 0.04 F1) are within that noise. The six-run sweep is meant to give error bars.
- Any claim about hard duplicates (acronyms, translations): mined pairs were selected by MiniLM similarity of 0.60 or
  higher, which favors near-identical names, and duplicates with very different strings are probably under-sampled.
- Judge accuracy beyond the 30 hand-labeled pairs (29 of 29 swap-consistent matched) and the random-pair check
  (98% of 200 random pairs labeled unrelated).

## End-to-end demo: collapsing duplicate names into clusters (`cluster_names.py`)

All 6,148 distinct institution names, merged into clusters when the detector scores a pair at or above the train-chosen
threshold; canonical name = the spelling used by the most repositories. Hierarchy is not merged (parent links are future work).

| | Fuzzy (>=0.885) | MiniLM (>=0.916) |
|---|---|---|
| Names -> clusters | 6,148 -> 5,507 (10.4% fewer) | 6,148 -> 5,491 (10.7% fewer) |
| Duplicates merged, test (23 pairs) | 74% | 87% |
| Hierarchy pairs wrongly merged, test (29) | 3% | 21% |
| Hierarchy wrongly merged, all judged (444) | 4% | 14% |
| Unrelated wrongly merged, all judged (1,290) | 1% | 3% |

- Merges inspected by eye (12 random fuzzy merges): 11 correct; the one error merged "NASA, Earth Science" into "NASA".
- MiniLM merges more duplicates but over-merges sub-units into parents (e.g. a department's Office of Science into the
  department, NCAR into UCAR) and sometimes unrelated institutes.
- **The audit understates over-merging.** It only counts pairs the judge labeled. Fuzzy's largest cluster merges 11
  different NIH institutes (siblings), which were never judged. The "all judged" rows are optimistic because the
  thresholds were tuned on the train part of that data.
- Output tables: `data/normalized_names_fuzzy.csv`, `data/normalized_names_minilm.csv`.

## Fine-tuning sweep on Negishi (6 runs: lr 1e-5 / 2e-5 / 5e-5 x seeds 1, 2; same 82-pair test set)

| lr | seed 1 F1 / AP | seed 2 F1 / AP |
|---|---|---|
| 1e-5 | 0.73 / 0.89 | 0.80 / 0.89 |
| 2e-5 | 0.78 / 0.88 | 0.78 / 0.89 |
| 5e-5 | 0.78 / 0.89 | 0.82 / 0.90 |

All runs: F1 mean 0.78 (range 0.73 to 0.82), AP mean 0.89 (range 0.88 to 0.90).
Baselines on the same split: off-the-shelf MiniLM F1 0.82 / AP 0.91; fuzzy F1 0.81 / AP 0.94.

**Reading:** fine-tuning did not improve over the off-the-shelf model. AP is threshold-free and all six runs are at or
below off-the-shelf; F1 varies by about 0.07 between seeds of one setting because the test set has only 23 duplicates.
Not tested: more seeds, other losses, a training set richer in hard duplicates (acronyms, translations), more labels.
The sweep ran on the split built from 2,290 judged pairs; later labels (2,400 mined by Oct 14) are not included.

## Error analysis and robustness check (`error_analysis.py`, `robustness_check.py`)

- On the 82-pair test set, fuzzy misses 6 of 23 duplicates and raises 2 false alarms; off-the-shelf MiniLM misses 3 and
  raises 6; the fine-tuned model (lr 2e-5, seed 1) misses 3 and raises 8. Fuzzy misses duplicates with added or reordered
  words; MiniLM scores sub-unit/parent pairs at 0.93-0.97 and flags them as duplicates.
- Averaging the fuzzy and MiniLM scores gave F1 0.87 / AP 0.95 on that test set, but across 10 random leakage-safe splits
  it averages F1 0.83 (+/-0.05) vs 0.80 (fuzzy) and 0.78 (MiniLM), beating both on F1 in 5/10 splits and on AP in 3/10.
  The gain is smaller than the spread; do not claim it as an improvement. (Splits reuse the same pairs, so they are not
  independent.) Only this one combination (a plain average) was tried.
- Fuzzy-alone F1 ranges from 0.68 to 0.92 across splits: a single ~80-pair test set is unreliable.
- Some near-identical pairs received different judge labels (duplicate vs hierarchy), so judge label noise caps every score.
