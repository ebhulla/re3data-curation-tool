Re3data, the largest registry of research data repositories (over 3,500), is curated by hand, often once, while the repositories keep changing. Its metadata can therefore be inconsistent or out of date, and nobody has measured by how much, which affects researchers who must trust it. This project has two aims: to measure re3data's accuracy by comparing a sample of records with each repository's own website, and to build an AI audit tool that proposes corrections for a human editor to verify. Both are in progress.

We report one working component: cleaning 6,148 institution name spellings. A research center inside a university is a different entity from the university, yet its name contains the university's, so simple text rules cannot tell duplicates from hierarchies. An AI language model labeled 2,290 name pairs, checking each in both orders; it matched all 29 consistent hand-labeled pairs. On 82 held-out pairs, string matching and meaning-based embeddings both found duplicates well (F1 about 0.8; 1.0 is perfect) but erred in opposite ways: embeddings merged sub-units into parents, and fine-tuning did not help. Results are preliminary. We recommend flagging parent-child pairs for human review, not merging them automatically; next we test the audit tool within the editorial workflow.
