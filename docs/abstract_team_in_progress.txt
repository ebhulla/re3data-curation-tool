Re3data indexes more than 3,500 research data repositories. Its records are curated by hand, often once, while the repositories keep changing, so metadata becomes inconsistent, incomplete or out of date, and nobody has measured by how much. This project has two aims: (1) to measure metadata accuracy in re3data by comparing a sample of records with each repository's own website, and (2) to build an AI audit engine that retrieves evidence from those pages and proposes a verdict for each field (confirmed, contradicted, new information, no evidence) for a human editor to verify. Both are in progress.

One component is already working: institution names. True duplicates look like legitimate hierarchies, such as a research center inside a university, and a substring rule cannot separate them (F1 0.49). We labeled name pairs with an LLM judge that checks each pair in both name orders (29 of 29 consistent hand labels matched). On 2,290 judged pairs, with 82 held out and no institution in two folds, fuzzy matching (F1 0.81) and sentence embeddings (F1 0.82) both find duplicates but fail in opposite ways: embeddings merge sub-units into their parents, and fine-tuning did not fix this. These results are preliminary: the test set is small and labels come from an LLM judge.

Next we will evaluate the audit engine against the website gold standard and examine how it can fit the re3data editorial workflow while keeping human judgement.
