Title (suggested): Duplicate or Parent? Normalizing Institution Names in the re3data Registry

re3data lists about 3,500 research data repositories whose records contain 6,148 distinct institution name strings. Cleaning them is harder than merging near-identical strings: a research center inside a university is a different entity from the university, yet its name often contains the parent's. A substring rule cannot separate duplicates from such hierarchies (F1 0.49), and matching to the ROR registry is not enough: only 16% of 200 sampled name pairs sharing a ROR record were true duplicates; the rest were mostly sibling or parent-child sub-units.

We labeled name pairs with an LLM judge that judges each pair in both name orders and keeps only consistent labels (Zheng et al., 2023). It matched 29 of 29 consistent hand-labeled pairs and called 98% of 200 random pairs unrelated. On 2,290 judged pairs, evaluated on an 82-pair held-out test set (23 duplicates) in which no institution appears in two folds, fuzzy string matching (F1 0.81) and off-the-shelf MiniLM embeddings (F1 0.82) score similarly but fail in opposite ways: fuzzy misses names with added words, while MiniLM treats a sub-unit and its parent as near-duplicates (14% of 444 judged hierarchy pairs wrongly merged vs 4% for fuzzy). Fine-tuning MiniLM did not help (six runs: mean F1 0.78, range 0.73-0.82; average precision 0.89 vs 0.91). Averaging the two scores gave a small, unreliable gain across 10 random splits (F1 0.83 vs 0.80). Merging by fuzzy match reduces 6,148 names to 5,507 clusters.

Results are preliminary: the test set is small, labels come from an LLM judge, and the pair sample favors similar-looking names. Separating duplicates from hierarchies is the central difficulty for automated metadata curation.
